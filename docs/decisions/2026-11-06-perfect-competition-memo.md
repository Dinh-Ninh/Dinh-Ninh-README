# Perfect Competition — Recommendation



**Recommendation.**



The farm should plant 10 beds of tomatoes, 20 beds of carrot, and 30 beds of mesclun. This plan is expected to earn $42,761.66 in profit and use 60 out of 64 beds. 



## Why 



In my first hypothesis for stage 1, my plan was to plant 12 beds of tomatoes, 20 beds of carrots, and 30 beds of mesclun. However, based on the model the farm should stop at 10 beds of tomatoes. The 10th and 11th beds would cost $8,248.59 and $9,390.72 to produce, which is more than its selling price of $8,800. The profit from tomatoes production is the highest, but as we plant more beds the labor costs also rise. This will make additional tomatoes production become expensive. 



## The judgment call


The farm should consider increasing the limits for other plants as well (carrots, mesclun). One more carrot bed could add $352.49 to profit. One more mesclun bed would add about $246. Carrots offer more return on revenue, so I recommend to expand its limit.



## What would change my answer



If in the future the market prices for the plants increased, planting more tomatoes would make you more profitable. For example, the 11th bed of tomato $9,390.72 to produce, so a higher selling price could make that bed worth planting more. Other factors could also change the best planting plan are labor costs and limits on each crop.
